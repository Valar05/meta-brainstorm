# Contact -> Consequence -> Adapt

**Date:** 2026-09-14
**Status:** active doctrine / candidate invariant beneath changing taste

Drew's surface taste varies wildly from day to day. Genre, tone, medium, scale, and aesthetic are therefore poor candidates for a fixed preference model.

A stronger recurring structure is:

```text
CONTACT -> CONSEQUENCE -> ADAPT
```

## Contact

The person touches the system, idea, object, world, or material directly enough to matter. Contact is not passive exposure. It is an intervention, question, move, choice, gesture, attack, edit, test, or other meaningful perturbation.

## Consequence

The world answers. The answer must be legible enough that the person can tell what their contact changed. Consequence may be physical, systemic, narrative, tactical, aesthetic, emotional, or informational, but it cannot be decorative noise.

## Adapt

The next action changes because of what happened. The participant, tool, system, or all three incorporate the consequence into the next move.

This produces a causal loop rather than a content preference:

```text
CONTACT
   ↓
CONSEQUENCE
   ↓
ADAPT
   ↓
NEW CONTACT
```

The loop can survive radical changes in surface taste. A vehicle game, procedural animation experiment, tactical model, creative tool, story system, or physical craft can all satisfy it while looking nothing alike.

## Taste implication

Do not freeze Drew's taste into a static genre profile. Treat day-to-day taste as weather. `CONTACT -> CONSEQUENCE -> ADAPT` is a candidate for a slower-moving structural preference beneath that weather.

This is not yet proven universal. Preserve it as a hypothesis and test it against counterexamples.

## Design implication

A system trying to meet current taste should prefer fast, low-cost opportunities for contact and visible consequence, then use the resulting behavior as evidence for adaptation. Asking the person to fully describe today's taste in advance may be less informative than giving them something meaningful to touch.

## Compact form

> **Do not predict the whole desire. Offer contact, show consequence, adapt.**
